About

PhD Candidate in Public Policy · Sanford School of Public Policy, Duke University

I am a PhD candidate in Public Policy at Duke University’s Sanford School of Public Policy, where I am also completing an MA in Economics en route. I study political economy, political institutions, and economic development, with a regional focus on the Philippines and Southeast Asia. My goal is to understand the political and economic processes that shape differences in development across countries—and the policies that can help narrow them.

My dissertation asks when citizens hold corrupt politicians accountable. Using preregistered survey experiments, administrative data, and fieldwork across Philippine municipalities, I examine how the nature of corruption—how openly and shamelessly it is done—shapes moral outrage, collective action, and electoral punishment.

Before Duke, I earned a Master of Global Affairs from the University of Notre Dame as a Liu Institute Fellow and an AB in Political Science from the Ateneo de Manila University. I previously worked at the Philippine Department of Foreign Affairs’ Foreign Service Institute, at Innovations for Poverty Action, and as a researcher on World Bank–assisted projects.
